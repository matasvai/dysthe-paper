               Dysthe Solitary Waves and the Published Tests
                            Literature review and computational audit

                     27 October 2026; revised after the cluster-results push


                                                 Abstract
         The defensible research target is to determine which resolved water-wave Dysthe solitary
     profiles remain close to their symmetry orbits over a measured propagation interval, and whether
     neural approximations reproduce that behavior. Two September 2026 preprints refine the
     theoretical context, but neither supplies an orbital-stability theorem for the implemented periodic
     free-surface model. The newly pushed cluster snapshot reports qualified periodic references
     through ξ = 64, but none of 24 evaluated neural fits passes the combined ξ = 4 gates; 12 random-
     field fits have execution errors. Detailed reference and failure records remain unpublished. A
     fresh projection audit shows that copying the generic-packet neural cutoff to the solitary-wave
     domain would impose a 66.1% relative H 2 error before optimization. This report distinguishes
     literature results, published measurements, local historical evidence and proposed tests.


1    The question and the exact equation
The project’s original question is whether solutions remain smooth over increasing propagation
distance. Solitary waves provide a controlled family with which to investigate this question, but
existence of one traveling profile does not establish global existence for arbitrary initial data.
   The active model evolves a scaled complex free-surface envelope u(ξ, τ ) in downstream distance
ξ, with retarded-time coordinate τ :

This is the spatial Fedele–Dutykh free-surface convention [1]. Its model identifier is water-wave-
dysthe-spatial-v1. The Fourier multiplier of |Dτ | is |k| and that of ∂τ is ik; thus H∂τ = |Dτ |.
The potential-envelope equation, a temporal equation, and a two-dimensional model are different
contracts. Their results cannot be imported by name alone.

2    What the current literature establishes
Direct numerical precedent. Fedele and Dutykh constructed spatial Dysthe traveling waves
with a Petviashvili iteration and studied their collisions numerically [1]. Their observed inelastic
interactions are an established benchmark, not a new discovery to claim from this project. Con-
vergence of that profile iteration is not dynamical stability. Earlier perturbative work by Akylas
described how higher-order modulation changes an initially NLS-like solitary envelope, including
amplitude and speed adjustments in the studied regime [2]. This motivates comparing an exact
NLS inlet with a corrected Dysthe profile.

A 2026 existence result, with a coefficient restriction. Esfahani, Kairzhan and Karazym’s
preprint, revised 21 September 2026, proves parameter-dependent traveling-wave existence and
nonexistence for a general nonlocal derivative NLS family [3]. In their convention,

                     iut − uxx − b|u|2 u + iα|u|2 ux + iβu2 ūx + γu∂x H(|u|2 ) = 0.

Matching (1.1) gives
                                     (b, α, β, γ) = (1, 8ϵ, 2ϵ, −2ϵ).
Their main existence theorems assume γ ≥ 0; their other negative-γ existence regime requires
a vanishing reduced quintic coefficient, whereas ours is B = −35ϵ2 /4. Hence those results do
not directly prove existence for this branch. They also explicitly leave stability and instability
unaddressed. This is a useful analytical framework, not a certificate for the current solver.

A sharper 2026 obstruction to NLS-type integrability. Sutherland and Yim’s 19 September
2026 preprint computes resonant six-wave scattering in Hamiltonian Dysthe models [4]. Its spatial
free-surface case is connected to our coefficients by the Fedele–Dutykh gauge transformation,
including the induced quintic term. Under its stated formal-functional and regularity assumptions,
the result excludes an infinite conserved hierarchy with independent regular C 3 quadratic leading
symbols beyond span{1, k, k 2 }. It does not exclude every conceivable notion of integrability, solitary
profiles, or stable propagation. The analysis uses continuous Fourier variables on the line; it is not a
periodic-box stability theorem. Both 2026 papers are preprints, not verified journal publications in
the records inspected.

Coherent events are broader than exact solitary waves. Schober and Islas’s 2025 study
uses Floquet spectra to examine soliton-like rogue events in conservative and dissipative higher-order
NLS settings [5]. Its publisher abstract was accessible; its full text was not inspected here. Tikan
et al. connect random narrowband water-wave fields, coherent-pulse signatures and nonlinear
dispersion measurements with NLS/Dysthe/Euler comparisons [6]. These support later random-
input diagnostics, not a claim that every large peak is a soliton. The supplied 2026 Schober–Islas
preprint concerns weak viscosity and mean-flow damping [7]; such terms require a separately
versioned extension, and are not part of (1.1).

3     Four claims that must remain separate
    1. Profile existence: a nontrivial Q solves the traveling-wave equation. A small computed
       residual is numerical evidence; an existence theorem needs an analytical or validated numerical
       argument.
    2. Spectral stability: the correct linearized evolution has no growing modes in a specified
       function space. A finite matrix calculation must be checked against resolution and domain
       changes.
    3. Finite-distance robustness: selected small perturbations stay close to the traveling-wave
       orbit over the tested interval. This can be measured with resolved simulations.
    4. Global orbital stability or global regularity: a uniform statement for all sufficiently
       small perturbations, or continuation for general data at arbitrary distance. The present tests
       do not establish either statement.
   The phrase soliton is used variably in this literature. Here, “solitary wave” describes a persistent
localized traveling profile; “integrable soliton” additionally invokes the special scattering structure of
an integrable model. Nonintegrability does not imply that every solitary wave is unstable. Likewise,
a transient focusing event or a breather need not be a traveling profile.
   The small-data global well-posedness and scattering theorem of Mosincat, Pilod and Saut concerns
a Dysthe equation on R2 [8]. Its dimensional and evolution setting differs from this one-coordinate
periodic spatial solver. It cannot be presented as a theorem covering our solitary profiles or arbitrary
large-amplitude data. No directly applicable orbital-stability theorem for the exact implemented
model was found in the sources reviewed; this is a literature search conclusion, not a proof that no
such result exists.

3.1    What “long term” means operationally
There is no universal terminal value of ξ, amplitude or H 2 norm that establishes long-term stability.
Record the actual interval together with natural propagation scales. With A0 = ∥u(0)∥∞ , the
current campaign uses
                                            Nnl (Ξ) = A20 Ξ
as an initial-peak nonlinear-distance convention. For a traveling wave also record |µ|Ξ/(2π) phase
cycles and the shift sΞ relative to its width. For the stored solitary profile, A0 = 1.958116: the
completed Ξ = 0.1 check covers 0.3834 nonlinear lengths, whereas the proposed Ξ = 20 study would
cover 76.68. This is a meaningful increase in scope, not an existing result.
   In the model normalization, ξ = ϵ2 k0 X and τ = ϵ(2k0 X − ω0 T ). Thus physical distance is
X = ξ/(ϵ2 k0 ) once the carrier is specified. The approximation need not remain physically accurate
at arbitrarily large X, even if its numerical solution stays smooth. Track effective steepness and
spectral bandwidth; use water-wave experiments or higher-fidelity water-wave models for physical
validation.

4.2    What the existing results imply for method selection
The slab PINN is the most promising tested neural method for the current per-inlet task. It is
not yet the fastest solver. Recorded median fitting time was about 1.26 seconds per inlet, versus
about 0.0294 seconds for a coarse numerical solve through ξ = 2. The latter had worst test H 1
error about 4.62 × 10−7 . These preliminary CPU timings were not an isolated, matched-accuracy
benchmark; some training overlapped and setup/audit costs were excluded. They do not establish
neural speedup.
   The trained FNO and PINO are not reliable long-distance predictors: neither passed all test
gates even through ξ = 0.5. Their ξ = 4 errors exceeded 100%. Twelve operator-training inlets
and a small budget are insufficient to draw a general conclusion about FNO, but these particular
checkpoints should not be promoted as a solver. NLS warm-starting also failed to improve the
aggregate PINN result under this budget.
   Three numerical validation fields were independently refined through ξ = 16; recorded sampled
H 2 norms remained finite, with maximum approximately 4.224. These are finite-distance periodic
observations. The new cluster snapshot extends the recorded numerical coverage below; neither set
establishes solitary-wave stability or continuum existence.

4.5   What the new results mean for the research goal
Derivative accuracy is the decisive new issue. Eleven of the 24 evaluated fits pass the
earlier CPU pilot’s L2 , H 1 and peak criteria, including all six evaluated test-sideband fits. Their
individual sampled_interval_pass flags are true because that older flag excludes H 2 . All 24 fail
the long-distance gate that includes H 2 ; for those eleven, the extra derivative criterion causes
rejection. Use the full campaign status, not the older flag, when reporting success. Initial gate
violations occur between ξ = 0.30 and 1.10, although each evaluated fit was trained through 4.
   This is directly relevant to the smoothness objective: a good envelope or peak prediction can
conceal inaccurate derivatives. An H 2 error of 22.1% is an approximation error relative to the
reference, not a 22.1% growth in the true norm and not evidence of a singularity. Its cause cannot
be inferred from aggregate errors alone. Inspect per-mode error and residuals to distinguish spurious
high-frequency content, truncation, phase drift and optimization error. The earlier CPU result and
this run differ in inlets, horizon, network, bandwidth, budget and acceptance criteria; the change
cannot be attributed to GPU execution.

Periodic qualification and isolated packets have different limits. The summary records
reference qualification through 64, but all packet domain checks fail at 8. Thus the recorded periodic
numerical evidence extends much farther than the declared doubled-domain agreement test, which
passes only through 4. This is not a whole-line certificate. The detailed records are needed to
distinguish finite-box interaction from failed refinement. Likewise, sensitivity qualification through
16 is not a bound on the size of amplification: it says the prescribed response comparisons passed.
The actual norms, amplification curves and qualification margins are not in the uploaded summary.

Execution and provenance limits. All 24 completed PINN records include positive GPU
allocations, with reported peak allocated tensor memory 94,955,520 bytes (90.56 MiB). Their
median recorded optimization time is 62.61 seconds (range 47.63–67.44). This is not total device
memory or a matched CPU/GPU speed comparison. CUDA 12.8 and PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128 are
recorded. The exact GPU model, node and scheduler allocation are not in the published records;
the declared gpu2 campaign targets A100 hardware. Job and runtime-lock files are only hash-listed.
The random-fit exception logs are absent, so neither memory exhaustion nor an optimizer failure
can be asserted as their cause.

Next decision. Retrieve the detailed numerical result.json files, the PINN H 2 traces and
tasks/pinn-024.json through pinn-035.json before diagnosing the missing random fits. Use
validation cases to test shorter slabs, resolved bandwidth and optimization settings under controlled
budgets. Do not automatically extend failed fits to larger distances. Freeze any selected repair
before evaluation on a new untouched test set. These generic-wave results inform the learning
method; they still do not test solitary-wave stability.

6    A staged solitary-wave experiment with explicit decisions
The following extension is proposed, not already present in the pushed campaign. It begins with
the existing µ = 2, s = 0 branch and ϵ = 0.025, 0.05. Broaden the branch only after the first cases
qualify. Avoid claiming novelty for solitary-wave construction or collision inelasticity alone; both
have established precedents.

S1. Construct and qualify the profile. Goal: distinguish an actual Dysthe traveling solution
from an NLS guess. Continue from ϵ = 0 and evaluate the full complex traveling residual on an
oversampled grid. Refine profile grid and domain separately; recompute instead of padding. Record
residual, projection floors, spectral and boundary tails, peak and mean flow. The local residual gate
is 10−6 ; require refinement agreement below the perturbation response. Failure means unresolved,
not absent. The analytic NLS control is exact on the line; its periodic-box use needs domain checks.
Refine propagation step h/2 separately in S2.

S2. Measure persistence and perturbation response. Goal: distinguish shape deformation
from position and phase drift. Start with an unperturbed control and signed localized amplitude,
phase and odd-amplitude perturbations. Normalize or report their actual H 1 sizes. Use sizes 10−3
and 5 × 10−4 , and checkpoints such as Ξ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 20, subject to reference/domain qualification.
Save densely enough to resolve focusing and transient growth; add samples when a peak is missed.
The evolved control measures numerical drift. The local plan saves only 21 stations through 20;
increase sampling before interpreting event timing.

S3. Connect growth to a linearized mechanism. Goal: test whether observed perturbation
growth agrees with a converged linear instability. In the moving/rotating frame the operator is
real-linear:
                                 Av = DF (Q)v + iµv + s∂τ v.
It includes variations through complex conjugation and the nonlocal mean flow. Verify the approxi-
mate neutral directions iQ and Q′ , then refine grid and domain and compare any positive growth
rate with nonlinear propagation while the perturbation remains small. Purely imaginary computed
eigenvalues do not prove nonlinear stability; transient growth and unresolved spectrum remain
possible. This operator is not currently implemented.

S4. Test a neural method on already qualified cases. Goal: determine whether the selected
slab PINN reproduces the numerical wave’s drift, deformation and radiation. Set the Fourier cutoff
from the profile and propagated spectral tails, rather than copying K = 48. Retain the original field
gates, add phase/position observables, and apply a separate response gate before making sensitivity
claims. Compare a shorter slab and higher bandwidth at matched, recorded optimization cost.
Preserve every failed case and seed.

S5. Collisions and emergence, after S1–S4. Goal: reproduce known interactions before
testing learned predictions. Compare separated Dysthe profiles with NLS at matched relative phase,
separation and speed; measure outgoing profiles and radiation with box refinement. Motion in
retarded time is not counterpropagation of physical carriers. Later, fit generic-packet peaks to
the qualified branch and check traveling residual and lifetime. This does not establish a universal
soliton-resolution conjecture.

grid/step/domain refinement and increasing spectral bandwidth. A rising tail at the cutoff usually
first establishes under-resolution. Distinguish that from optimization failure, boundary return and
physical-model breakdown. Failure to observe growth likewise does not prove global smoothness.

8     How PINNs and FNOs should be used here
8.1   Slab PINN: a separately fitted solution on successive intervals
A slab PINN solves [ξj , ξj+1 ], passes its predicted endpoint to the next slab, and repeats. The
current implementation uses a network in ξ that outputs complex Fourier coefficients in τ ; this
enforces periodicity and permits complete-slice evaluation of |Dτ |(|u|2 ). It does not sample the
nonlocal term from disconnected points. Numerical labels are reserved for auditing; slab endpoints
must not be reset to the reference during evaluation.
   Sequence training has methodological support in PINN failure-mode work [10]. Wang et al.’s
causal weighting is a related but distinct algorithm [11]: sequential slabs alone are not their specific
residual weighting rule. Any added weighting should be a separately tested ablation. Residual
minimization measures equation fit over the fitted interval, not forecast generalization or an existence
certificate.
   For solitary waves, first compare the existing spectral correction against a known moving/rotating
reference representation, both with the same inlet and full residual. A moving frame may re-
duce phase/translation learning burden, but it must not freeze amplitude or suppress admissible
perturbations. The benefit is a hypothesis to measure, not an established Dysthe result.

8.2   FNO: learn across initial functions, not one profile
FNO learns an operator from a family of functions [12]; duplicating one profile with many snapshots
does not provide many independent inlets. A useful new dataset would span qualified branch
parameters, global phase, translations, and controlled amplitude/phase/radiative perturbations,
then include generic packet controls. Keep all snapshots, augmentations, signed perturbations and
refinements of a parent inlet in one train/validation/test group.
   Compare the same architecture trained with data loss against a PINO residual augmentation
[13], using equal data, seeds and recorded cost. Freeze the choice on validation fields before test
inspection. Evaluate short-step accuracy and autonomous rollout without reference resets. Separate
interpolation within the sampled branch from held-out amplitude/steepness ranges. More epochs
alone are not a supported remedy for the existing pilot failures.
   An ordinary FNO’s latent Fourier cutoff does not by itself strictly limit its output, because
local nonlinear layers can create higher frequencies. The projection floor in Section 5 applies to
the explicit output projector used in this project’s spectral models. Record latent modes, output
cutoff and grid separately. Phase/translation augmentation tests physical symmetries but should
not inflate the independent test count.

8.3   Priority and cost
Run reference qualification and the slab PINN extension first. Treat FNO recovery as a separate
controlled operator study. On A100, record data generation, optimization, validation, checkpoint
I/O, evaluation and peak memory; compare total cost at matched measured accuracy against the
Fourier solver. A cached neural forward pass is not the cost of obtaining a new per-inlet PINN
solution.

The near-term question is:

      For a specified family of resolved spatial free-surface Dysthe solitary waves, how do
      perturbation growth, phase/position drift and radiation depend on steepness and propa-
      gation distance, and which of these behaviors can a slab PINN or an operator model
      reproduce within a measured error budget?

This can produce useful numerical evidence without promising a global existence proof. A subsequent
proof project could study small-ϵ continuation from NLS or validated a-posteriori existence, but
would need explicit function spaces, kernel removal, nonlocal estimates and validated inverse/tail
bounds. Neither a converged iteration nor an ML residual supplies these ingredients.
